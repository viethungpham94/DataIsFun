Messi and Ronaldo: Who Is the Real GOAT?

My attempt to deconstruct the GOAT debate in a pure non-partisan, pure data-driven manner, by breaking down different aspects of two of the greatest football players of all time.

Foreword

“
November 2026

Growing up in Vietnam, football was everywhere. I still remember the roar of entire neighbourhoods when we scored against Thailand in the 2003 SEA Games final, and the eerie, empty streets whenever people dragged their TVs out onto the sidewalks to watch Vietnam win another big match.

Funnily enough, my own obsession didn’t actually start until I played FIFA in high school. Then I moved to England for my A-levels, where I finally started playing the game myself (and quickly realised I suck), and spent every spare minute in the school IT lab rewatching highlights of Ibrahimović, Kaká, Messi, and Ronaldo. Today, football is easily one of my biggest passions.

Naturally, when deciding on a topic for my first analytics post, the GOAT debate came to mind immediately. Full disclosure: neither of them is actually my all-time favourite player. That honour belongs to a few: Son Heung-min (Asian pride, stands toe to toe as some of the best forwards in his time), Zlatan Ibrahimović (purely for the outrageous stunts) and Thomas Müller (a guy with zero flashy technique or blistering pace who just wins with his brain). Between Messi and Ronaldo however, I’ll admit I have a stronger admiration toward Messi, which is precisely why I need to be structured to keep personal bias in check.

To tackle this, I leaned on my consulting background by breaking a massive, messy question down into smaller, verifiable statements. That way, we can evaluate each sub-problem objectively.

At the same time, half the magic of football lies in its subjectivity: the banter, the punditry, and the endless what-ifs. Because both players are phenomenal in completely different systems, I also built a quick interactive tool so you can weigh the traits that matter most to you and define your own GOAT.

Football is a great playground for data work simply because that the public datasets are so rich. But it is not without limitations. The biggest challenges are data availability, robustness, and access, as more advanced big data are really only captured recently, and many are gated behind expensive paywall, or is completely private. That means, I have to pick and choose the right dataset, and note certain assumptions that those datasets might bring.

While my background is in analytics and consulting rather than dedicated sports data science, I’m not here to reinvent the wheel in a space already full of brilliant football analysts. I just wanted to apply a structured, hypothesis-free lens to the numbers, stay open-minded, and see where the data actually led.

In the end, this is a much deeper rabbit hole than I expected. I eventually had to force myself to draw a line under the scope because new questions kept sprouting, often faster than data availability. There is an endless ocean of things to analyse in football. While I have other topics I want to explore next, I’ll definitely be coming back to the pitch every now and then.

What The Data is Saying

Remark (Click to view / hide)

Across 1,915 senior goals, 774 assists, and 25 shared seasons across club and international football:

  • Messi leads in all-round playmaking (+160 assists, 2.3× Big Chances Created), non-penalty scoring (+24 NPG), finishing efficiency (+37.95 Opta 13-season league xG surplus margin), total attacking volume (1,404 vs 1,286 G+A), ball progression (3,093 vs 1,655 dribbles), goals vs FIFA Top-20 nations (34 vs 29), and FIFA World Cup knockout delivery (7 vs 1 knockout goals; 21 vs 11 overall).
  • Ronaldo leads in gross career volume (+43 gross goals: 979 vs 936), penalty execution (+67 converted PKs: 186 vs 119), aerial duel dominance (+616 Opta aerial duels won; +103 headers), UEFA Champions League scoring crowns (+1) & titles (5 vs 4), and carried squads with lower average on-pitch teammate market value (€33.2M vs €37.5M).
  • Knockout game-winners stand at an exact dead heat: 44–44 career game-winning goals in knockout matches (while Messi leads total knockout goals 152 vs 133).
