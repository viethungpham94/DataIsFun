Immigration in the UK: Flow, Stock, Crime, Economy & Social Impact

Part 1 · Overall Trends (Flow & Stock) → Part 2 · Are They Causing Crime? → Part 3 · Are They Causing an Economic Problem? → Part 4 · Are They Blending In or Causing Social Problems? → Part 5 · Do the Solutions Work?

Data Availability Notes: Requested Metrics Without Public UK Open Data
  • Immigrant Income Percentiles & Within-Group Range (P10 / P25 / P75 / P90): HMRC’s public PAYE RTI nationality release publishes median monthly pay by nationality (UK, EU, Non-EU, and top 50 nationalities — charted in Section 1.2), but does not publish within-group income percentiles or interquartile ranges by nationality in open tables (restricted to Secure Research Service / Integrated Data Service).
  • Age- & Sex-Adjusted Crime / Custody Rates by Ethnicity & Religion: MoJ and Home Office publish raw custody/arrest shares by ethnicity and religion without cross-tabulated age-sex standardised rates or visa-route breakdowns; comparisons in Section 2.2 are therefore unadjusted.
  • Annual Ethnicity & Religion Resident Populations (Inter-Census): Official England & Wales ethnicity and religion shares are collected decennially (Census 2011 and 2021), so annual 2015–2026 prison series cannot be divided by annual resident denominators.
  • Pre-2019 Route-Level Net Migration Breakdown: ONS LTIM Table 4b (Work Main, Study, Asylum) starts in YE Dec 2019, so workforce-filtered net migration in Section 1.2 begins in 2019 while total net migration as % of the 16–64 workforce spans 1971–2025.
  • EU Settlement Scheme (EUSS) Pre-Settled Status vs Visa Stock: Home Office Migrant Journey valid-leave stock (1.1, 1.2, 1.3) covers people granted visa leave from 2008 onwards who have not yet expired or settled (ILR), but excludes EU/EEA citizens holding temporary Pre-Settled Status under the separate EUSS register.

Part 1 · Overall Trends (Flow & Stock)

Immigration in the Past & Current 'On Visa' Stock (1964–2025)

How has UK migration evolved over 62 years, does Non-EU+ net migration include visa expiries and departures, and what drove the 2020–2021 step-change?

Does Non-EU+ Net Migration include people leaving when visas expire or after study? Yes — Net Migration = Inflows minus Outflows (including departing students and workers whose visas expire). For Non-EU+ nationals, ONS LTIM records 182k departures in 2019, dipping to 88k in 2021 during COVID, then tripling to 278k departures in 2025 as 2021–2023 cohorts left. Shaded chart bands show the UK governing party: Red = Labour, Blue = Conservative, Indigo = Coalition.
Over 62 years (1964–2025), UK net migration swung from negative outflows (1964–1982) to ~200k–300k (2004–2019), before the post-2021 spike above 900k.

As a share of total UK population, net migration peaked at 1.32% in 2022 — roughly triple the 2004–2019 average (~0.3%–0.5%).

What drove the 2020–2021 break: Study, Health & Care (Feb 2022), and Humanitarian schemes (Ukraine / BN(O)) drove the step-change over 2019.
