Issue 03 · Graphs-First Preview

The Benefit and Cost of AI: Are the Gains Worth the Price?

Testing AI compute scale, medical & pricing benefits, circular hyperscaler capital risks, and human-vs-AI cost parity against audited SEC EDGAR, Epoch AI, OWID, US FDA, and US BLS datasets.

Methodological Guardrail: First-Party AI Vendor Scrutiny & Coverage Gaps
  • First-Party Vendor Scrutiny: Datasets published directly by AI labs (e.g., Anthropic Economic Index) carry commercial self-selection bias and are excluded as primary baselines here.
  • Circular Economy & Subsidy Reality: Commercial API token list prices are juxtaposed against audited SEC EDGAR hyperscaler CapEx, NVIDIA revenue, and Net PP&E depreciation to distinguish unit efficiency from balance-sheet subsidies.
  • Historical Case Study Lens (Topic 3C): Electronic computers replacing human computers, the 1987 Solow Paradox productivity lag, and the 2000 dot-com IT CapEx peak provide empirical baselines for evaluating AI's current trajectory.

Coverage Note · Robotics & Mathematics Caveat

Robotics: US Census BTOS shows physical robotics process automation remains below 1% of US businesses compared with customer-facing software, and open-licensed global robot installation tables are not yet mirrored in OWID. Mathematics: Rapid gains on competition benchmarks such as MATH-500 reflect pattern retrieval and reasoning on known problems rather than verified, novel mathematical discovery.

Algorithmic Dependency, Media Narratives & Data Gap

As commercial labs produce the overwhelming majority of frontier models, businesses and citizens increasingly rely on opaque ranking, search, and recommendation algorithms controlled by a handful of providers. Meanwhile, media narratives routinely polarise between existential-risk headlines and productivity hype; independent, open-licensed datasets quantifying AI media sentiment bias are not yet available in our primary open registries.
